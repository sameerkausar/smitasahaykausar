My path to medicine began with engineering. As a bioengineering student at UC San Diego, I worked on projects ranging from computational research in rare neurologic diseases to the development of a low-cost HIV viral load device for a clinic in Tijuana. I learned how to think about complex problems and, more importantly, how research can improve people’s lives.

During my M.D./Ph.D. training at the University of Toledo, I applied my background in biochemical laboratory techniques and bioinformatics to the study of neuropsychiatric illness. I examined how changes in metabolism and cellular signaling contribute to the pathophysiology of schizophrenia, major depression, and suicide, and used those findings to explore novel therapeutic targets. What kept me interested was not one method or disease, but the process of following a question wherever it led.

In medical school, I found that same intellectual range in otolaryngology-head and neck surgery. My background in neuroscience gave me the foundation to understand how closely head and neck function is tied to the nervous system, while surgery offered a new way to solve problems and care for patients. Several years of service through humanitarian work and free clinics have also shaped the kind of physician I hope to become. I aim to continue building a career in which research informs clinical decisions, improves patients’ lives, and widens access to care.

Outside the lab and clinic

I enjoy staying physically active through high-intensity interval training (HIIT), yoga, and hiking. I grew up in Salt Lake City, Utah, where my love of the outdoors began. I enjoy traveling as a way to learn about other cultures and some of my favorite trips have been to Peru, France, and the Philippines. My husband and I have grown a short-term rental business around our shared love of travel.

Purinergic System Transcript Changes in the Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex in Suicide and Major Depressive Disorder

The first study to map the purinergic pathway across cortical layers of postmortem dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, comparing people with major depression who died by suicide to those who did not. Metabolism & signaling

Neuronal alterations in AKT isotype expression in schizophrenia

Here we measure AKT isotype expression in enriched pyramidal neurons rather than bulk brain tissue. All three AKT transcripts are elevated in schizophrenia despite earlier reports of unchanged protein levels. The increases are largely found in females and are independent of prior antipsychotic use.

Molecular Psychiatry, 2024

First Author
Bioinformatics, Metabolism & Signaling

Metabolic insights into Neuropsychiatric Illnesses and Ketogenic Therapies: A Transcriptomic View

In this study, we compare metabolic pathway gene expression across schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, and major depression to that in models of ketosis. Glycolysis, the Krebs cycle, and the electron transport chain are the most disrupted in all three illnesses and several genes move discordantly under ketogenic conditions. This suggests that ketone bodies may reverse some of these changes and partly explains their utility as a treatment option.

International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2024
PDF
First Author
Genetics, Metabolism and Signaling

Expression of WNT Signaling Genes in the Dorsolateral Prefrontal Cortex in Schizophrenia

This study pairs measurements of WNT pathway transcripts in postmortem dorsolateral prefrontal cortex with in silico data across published datasets. The WNT pathway governs cell growth, brain development, and synaptic plasticity. Region-level expression is unchanged, but cell-level data shows alterations and antipsychotic-specific effects, suggesting WNT changes depend on cell type.

Brain Sciences, 2024

Adenosine Receptor mRNA Expression in Frontal Cortical Neurons in Schizophrenia

This study measures the adenosine receptors A1 and A2A, which regulate glutamate and dopamine signaling, in pyramidal neurons isolated from postmortem anterior cingulate cortex. A1 is elevated in female subjects with schizophrenia, and A2A increases with dementia severity in females and males.

Metabolism & signaling

Activity of Protein Kinase A in the Frontal Cortex in Schizophrenia

The first study to measure protein kinase A (PKA) enzyme activity in the anterior cingulate and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex in schizophrenia, testing whether earlier predictions of raised PKA hold functionally. We find no difference at the region level, suggesting that any changes in PKA activity may be specific to particular cell types.
